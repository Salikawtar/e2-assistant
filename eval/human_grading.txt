  6.0 Bringing the E2 Plan into Effect. Bringing the E2 Plan into Effect 6. Within 12 months after the day on which an environmental emergency plan is required to be prepared under subsection 4(1), a responsible person must bring the plan into effect and submit a notice to the Minister that contains the information referred to in Schedule 4. The E2 Plan requires that a number of measures related to a facility’s prevention, preparedness and response to an environmental emergency be described. This includes the identification of elements such as: roles and responsibilities of key personnel, training, emergency response equipment, and advance public notification. Section 6 of the E2 Regulations requires that the E2 Plan be brought into effect within 12 months of the date that it is required to be prepared under subsection 4(1): - For uncontained E2 substances - within 12 months of meeting or exceeding the quantity threshold in Schedule 1 of the E2 Regulations for that substance - For E2 substances stored in a container system - within 12 months of meeting or exceeding both the substance quantity and the container capacity thresholds identified in Schedule 1 of the E2 Regulations. This means that the responsible person must take the necessary steps to advance past the mere completion of the E2 Plan to ensuring that the elements described therein are implemented and/or ready to be actioned. Specific examples of what this entails are described below in Table 6. Table 6. Requirements for Bringing an E2 Plan into Effect Section of E2 Requirement For Bringing the E2 Plan Into Effect Regulations 4(2)(g) - The preventative measures described in the E2 Plan (e.g., process safety management system, hazard control program, mitigative barriers, safety barriers, etc.) must be in place and functioning as intended.

CLAIM 9 of 12   (key P2#3)
==============================================================================

SENTENCE
  The environmental emergency plan must be site-specific, meaning that if a regulatee owns several facilities, they must prepare a plan and submit separate notices and reports for each facility [4].

EVIDENCE IT CITES
  (guidance) Technical Guidelines for the E2 Regulations, 2019 (v2.0), s. 5.0 (p. 26), part 1 of 2
  5.0 Environmental Emergency Plans. The E2 Regulations require facilities that meet or exceed established container capacity and / or quantity thresholds for E2 substances to develop an Environmental Emergency Plan (E2 Plan). The complexity of an E2 Plan may vary depending upon the circumstances, but the following basic factors always need to be taken into consideration: - The E2 Plan must be site specific. If a regulatee owns several facilities, they must prepare an E2 Plan and submit separate notices and reports for each facility. - A single E2 Plan may deal with one or more substances as long as it addresses the full range of potential hazards present on the site and the elements of prevention, preparedness, response and recovery for identified E2 scenarios. - Site specific training and emergency response equipment must be identified in the E2 Plan - The E2 Plan must contain a process for notification of environmental emergencies with members of the public who may be adversely affected. - Records of annual simulation exercises and any subsequent updates to the E2 Plan must be kept at the facility. Who Should Prepare an E2 Plan? An E2 Plan is required of any person who owns or has the charge, management or control of any of the regulated substances located at a facility, under certain conditions. To see if you are required to prepare, implement and carry out an E2 Plan, refer to Section 4 of these Guidelines. It is important to realize that it is the Notice Regarding the Preparation of an Environmental Emergency Plan (Schedule 3) and the Notice Regarding the Bringing into Effect of an Environmental Emergency Plan (Schedule 4) that are submitted to ECCC and NOT the E2 Plan itself. However, E2 Plans can be requested by ECCC’s Enforcement Branch to conduct a compliance inspection.
